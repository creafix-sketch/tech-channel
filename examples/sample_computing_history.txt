In 1945, engineers unveiled ENIAC, a giant machine filled with vacuum tubes and cables.
Alan Turing had already imagined a universal computing device that could follow any algorithm.
By the mid-1970s, the Altair 8800 sparked a hobbyist revolution in personal computing.
Steve Wozniak and Steve Jobs launched the Apple II in 1977, bringing color graphics to homes.
The IBM PC arrived in 1981 and quickly standardized the personal computer industry.
Then Tim Berners-Lee created the World Wide Web at CERN, connecting information across the planet.
Moore's Law and the rise of the integrated circuit made machines smaller, faster, and cheaper every year.
From Babbage's Analytical Engine to Silicon Valley, the story of computing is a story of bold ideas made real.
